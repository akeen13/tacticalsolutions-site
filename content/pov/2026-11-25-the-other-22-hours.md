A 24×7 SOC is two hours of attention spread across twenty-four.

That sentence sounds cynical. It is not. It is what every honest security director tells you over a beer. The roster is real. The shifts are real. The dashboards are real. But the thing the SOC is actually built for (a human paying attention to the right alert at the right moment) happens for a couple of hours a day, in bursts, when the senior analyst is in. The rest is queue.

The queue is the problem. Not the model. Not the SIEM. The queue.

Every security tool you bought in the last decade was sold to you with the same promise: better signal, less noise, faster triage. And every one of them, more or less, delivered. The signal is better. The noise is lower. The mean-time-to-detect graphs in your QBRs do trend down. None of it changed the fundamental shape of the work, which is that a human still has to look at every alert that survives the funnel and decide what to do, and there are not enough humans, and the ones you have are quitting because closing P5 tickets at 3 a.m. is not what they signed up for.

You know how this ends. You hire an MSSP. The MSSP also has a queue. The queue is now slightly less your problem, in the sense that the cost line moved from "salaries" to "services" on your spreadsheet. The Tuesday 2 a.m. anomaly still becomes Wednesday morning's ticket. You are still buying the appearance of coverage rather than coverage.

We built ProSentinel because the math finally changed.

For ten years, the bottleneck on autonomous security operations was that the models could not reason about an alert in context. They could classify, they could cluster, they could write a passable summary. They could not look at "user logs in from Lagos at 02:14, then SSO into the finance tenant, then export a CSV" and decide whether that was the CFO on holiday or the start of an incident. The decision required judgment under uncertainty, and judgment under uncertainty was the thing the models could not do.

That bottleneck is gone. Not in some hand-wavy "soon" sense. Right now, in production, on real security telemetry, models are good enough to make those calls, when they are wrapped in the right loop, given the right tools, and bounded by the right thresholds. ProSentinel itself is pre-cohort. This is the first time the loop has been worth building. The hard part is no longer the model. It is the integration. It is the playbook. It is the accountability layer that makes a CISO comfortable that the system will do the right thing at 2 a.m. and produce evidence that holds up under audit at 10.

That is what we built ProSentinel to be.

ProSentinel runs the closed loop on every alert that hits your stack. It correlates across the tools you already pay for. It runs your playbook. It checks identity, asset criticality, recent context, known-bad patterns. For everything below the threshold you set, it acts: isolates the host, kills the token, opens or closes the ticket, logs the evidence. For everything above, it escalates with a one-screen brief: what happened, what it did, what is left for you to decide.

The boundary is yours. The thresholds are yours. The playbooks are yours. The evidence is yours. ProSentinel is the operator that runs them when your operators are off the clock.

We are deliberately not in the business of selling "fully autonomous." Anyone who tells a security team they should hand over judgment to a model with no boundary is selling them something they will regret. ProSentinel runs autonomously inside the boundary you draw, and shows its work for everything it does. The autonomy is real. The boundary is non-negotiable.

What we hand back to your team is the other twenty-two hours. The shift coverage that used to be a roster problem becomes a configuration problem. The headcount you keep moves up the stack (incident response, threat hunting, hardening) and stops drowning in triage. The work that nobody wanted, nobody does. The work that needed humans, still gets them.

We are onboarding a first cohort of design partners now. Mid-market security teams that already pay for a SOC and feel the gap. MSSPs that want operator leverage instead of headcount. One conversation, one scope, one measurable outcome inside sixty days.

If your SOC is awake for two hours a day, we should talk about the other twenty-two.
